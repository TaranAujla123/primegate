The Practice · 03

Businesses sold with their real estate.

From $2M Transaction Value

By Taran Aujla, Salesperson

Owner-operated businesses sold together with the property they occupy. The transaction is two assets in one file: a going concern with its trade, equipment, goodwill, and operating history, and the underlying real estate that supports it. Both have to be valued, structured, and closed correctly for the deal to hold.

What this lane covers.

  • Plazas with anchor and in-line tenants, sold by the long-term landlord-operator.
  • Gas stations and c-stores, including branded and independent operations, with the underlying parcel.
  • Industrial and flex buildings owner-occupied by the operating business.
  • Specialty retail with established trade, brand, and customer base, sold to a continuing operator.
  • Small hospitality: restaurants, motels, boutique inns, country resorts, sold as going concerns with their real estate.

Who this is for.

Two sides, often in the same conversation.

  • Sellers. Owner-operators approaching retirement or planning a structured exit, often holding both the operating business and the underlying real estate, sometimes through related corporations.
  • Buyers. Continuing operators expanding, family offices, immigrant-investor capital, and holding companies seeking income property with an operating tenant attached.

What the practice does in this lane.

Files in this lane are slower-moving and more technically complex than residential or pure investment property. The work that produces a clean closing is the work done at the front of the file: pricing the two assets correctly, structuring the deal so each side's tax and legal exposure is known, and running diligence that catches the items that decide outcome.

  • Two-asset valuation. Real estate priced against comparable commercial sales. Going concern priced on multiple of stabilised earnings, normalised owner compensation, and quality of the trade. The combined ask reconciled, not just summed.
  • Lease and estoppel structure. Where there are tenants other than the owner-operator, the lease terms, renewal rights, deposit treatment, and estoppel positions are reviewed and the gaps surfaced before the listing.
  • HST and tax treatment. Whether the deal is structured as an asset purchase, a share purchase, or a hybrid, with the tax implications mapped on both sides and the right professionals brought in early.
  • Environmental flags. Phase I and Phase II decisions, fuel-handling and underground-tank history on gas station and industrial files, dry-cleaning and printing residue on retail and industrial, and the practical timeline for environmental clearance.
  • Confidentiality. Employees, suppliers, customers, and competitors should not learn of a pending sale until the seller chooses. The marketing, the diligence flow, and the offer process all run accordingly.
  • Buyer financing reality. SBA-equivalent commercial financing, vendor takeback structures, and the realistic credit profile of the buyer pool for the specific asset class.

Geographic focus.

Brampton, Mississauga, the Halton corridor, Hamilton, and Niagara for industrial, plaza, and gas station files. Cottage country and small hospitality across Muskoka, the Kawarthas, and Georgian Bay. Specialty retail across the GTA. Outside those geographies on the right file with the right counterparty.

Notes from the practice.

The deeper guide on this lane covers two-asset valuation, the going concern, the lease problem, working capital, confidentiality, and the buyer's financing reality. The questions worked through on every Ontario file where a business and its real estate are sold together.
