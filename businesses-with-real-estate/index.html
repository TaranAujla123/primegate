Businesses Sold With Their Real Estate.

Plazas · Gas Stations · Industrial & Flex · Specialty Retail · Small Hospitality

By Taran Aujla, Salesperson

Owner-operated businesses sold together with the real estate they occupy. Plazas, gas stations and c-stores, industrial and flex buildings, specialty retail, and small hospitality. Files where the deal spans both the operating business and the underlying property, and where the value sits in both. The mechanics that decide outcome are real estate diligence (title, environmental, lease estoppels), the asset-versus-share decision, HST treatment, and reconciliation of the operating numbers against what the property and the business can each carry.

What this lane covers.

  • Owner-operated businesses sold together with the property they occupy, where the real estate is part of the transaction and the operating business is part of the transaction.
  • Plazas, where the deal spans the underlying retail property and the in-place tenant covenants, with or without an anchor owner-operator unit.
  • Gas stations and c-stores with their site, where environmental diligence, fuel-supply contracts, and the underlying real estate are all part of the file.
  • Industrial and flex buildings owned and occupied by the operating business, where the property and the business sell as one transaction.
  • Specialty retail with owned premises, where the location, the goodwill, and the real estate are interlocked.
  • Small hospitality with the underlying real estate (motels, small inns, boutique hospitality), where the going-concern value sits on top of the property.

Who this is for.

  • Owner-operators preparing to exit a business they own together with its real estate, who want a transaction process that handles both pieces in one file.
  • Family-held operating businesses planning succession or a sale of the operating company together with the property it occupies.
  • Acquirers looking to buy a going concern along with its real estate, where the property is part of the underwriting.
  • Sellers and buyers who want the transaction structured so the substantive legal and tax decisions are handled by qualified professionals, not by the broker.

What the practice does in this lane.

A business sold with its real estate is two deals in one. The real estate transaction is the part the broker runs. The business sale (asset versus share, tax treatment, employment continuity, customer and supplier contracts, regulatory consents) is structured by your lawyer and accountant. The work the practice does ahead of the offer is to make sure both pieces fit together cleanly, so the transaction holds when it gets to documentation.

  • Real estate diligence. Title, easements, encroachments, zoning conformity, building condition, and any restrictions that affect the property's continued use by the operating business.
  • Lease estoppels and side leases. Where the operating business pays rent to a related landlord entity, where there are co-tenants in a plaza, or where the seller is severing the real estate from the business, the lease structure and estoppels are part of the file.
  • Environmental flags. Gas stations, dry cleaners, industrial sites, and any site with historical use that triggers Phase I or Phase II requirements. The practical question of what diligence the lender and the lawyer will require, and how that fits the closing timeline.
  • Asset-versus-share decision. The position-level discussion of which structure works for each side, with the substantive tax and liability analysis routed to the client’s accountants and lawyers. The broker role is to surface the decision early, not to make it.
  • HST treatment. Where the real estate component and the business component each trigger HST or qualify for an exemption, and how that affects the allocation of the purchase price.
  • Allocation of purchase price. Between the real estate and the operating business, with the substantive tax planning handled by accountants on both sides. The broker role is to surface the decision and reflect it in the agreement.
  • Operating numbers reconciliation. Working with the seller’s accountant to present the operating business in a form a buyer can underwrite, with the real estate component carried at a defensible value and the business component normalized.
  • Realistic buyer pool and process. Who the actual buyers are for a business sold together with its real estate, how they get reached, what their offer behaviour looks like, and what the negotiation will turn on.

Geographic focus.

The lane works most actively across the GTA and the Golden Horseshoe for plazas, industrial and flex buildings, and specialty retail with owned premises. Hamilton, Niagara, and Kitchener-Waterloo for owner-operated businesses with real estate, including small hospitality and gas stations and c-stores. Cottage country and Prince Edward County for boutique hospitality with the underlying real estate.

Notes from the practice.

The deeper guide on this lane runs the real estate diligence, the asset-versus-share decision, lease and environmental considerations, and the buyer-pool reality in the order each one matters.
