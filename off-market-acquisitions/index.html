The Practice · 01

Off-Market Acquisitions.

Power of Sale · Private Sales · Assignment Purchases · Estate Dispositions · Portfolio Additions

By Taran Aujla, Salesperson

A meaningful share of Ontario residential and multi-unit transactions closes without ever reaching MLS. Power of sale files, private sales between principals, assignment purchases, estate dispositions, and portfolio additions all move through channels that require a buyer-side practitioner who can read title, tenancy, financing, and closing risk properly. The value in these files is not a discount promised at the outset. It is a defensible offer built on what the file actually supports.

What this lane covers.

  • Power of sale purchases, where the file supports it, structured around the redemption window and the lender’s posture on the sale.
  • Private sales between principals, sourced brokerage-to-brokerage, where the seller has chosen to move outside the open market.
  • Assignment purchases, both pre-construction and completed-project, with the assignment agreement and builder consent read properly before the offer.
  • Estate dispositions, where an executor or estate trustee is selling and the sale timeline follows probate progress rather than market timing.
  • Portfolio additions for existing investors reallocating within Ontario, sourced against documented acquisition criteria.
  • Off-market residential and multi-unit that never surface on MLS, sourced through brokerage-to-brokerage channels.
  • Primarily Ontario, primarily the $500K to $3M range, with individual files above or below where the fundamentals warrant.

Who this is for.

  • Investors deploying capital into Ontario real estate acquisitions on a defined timeline.
  • Cash-ready buyers positioned to close without financing conditions where the file requires it.
  • Existing property owners adding to a portfolio, with acquisition criteria already documented.
  • Family capital reallocating into Ontario real estate positions with a defined deployment intent.
  • Buyers whose criteria (geography, asset type, budget, closing timeline) are consistent and can be matched against files as they surface.
  • Repeat clients who have worked through prior acquisitions with the practice.

What the practice does in this lane.

Off-market files close cleanly when the diligence work is done properly before the offer, not after. That is the work.

  • Documented acquisition criteria at intake. Geography, asset type, budget, closing timeline, financing pathway, and any specific structuring requirements captured at the outset. Matching against files as they surface is intentional, not opportunistic.
  • Title condition review. Registered restrictions, easements, liens, and anything on title that changes the offer, the closing math, or the closing timeline.
  • Tenancy and current use. For income files, the rent roll verified against bank deposits, every lease abstracted, and the current use compared against zoning and permitted use.
  • Financing pathway. Whether the acquisition can be financed on standard terms, requires a private lender bridge, or is a straight cash close. Confirmed before the offer, not assumed.
  • Offer structure. Conditional periods matched to the diligence required, deposit terms, closing dates, and the conditions worth insisting on for the file type.
  • Multiple representation disclosure. Where the same brokerage represents both sides of a transaction, multiple representation is disclosed and consented to in writing by both parties before any showing or offer, in accordance with TRESA.
  • Closing coordination. All offers and closings executed through HomeLife G1 Realty Inc., Brokerage, with your real estate lawyer in the loop from the outset.

Geographic focus.

The lane works most actively across Ontario’s GTA and adjacent development belt: Peel, Halton, Hamilton, York, Durham, Niagara, and Waterloo Region. Files in southwestern and central Ontario are considered as the fundamentals warrant. Cottage-country files across Muskoka, Haliburton, the Kawarthas, Prince Edward County, and Georgian Bay are considered when they fit an investor’s documented acquisition criteria.
