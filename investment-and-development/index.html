The Practice · 02

Investment and development property.

Land · Multi-Unit · Income · Cottage

By Taran Aujla, Salesperson

Property purchased for investment, development, or income. The category covers a wide range of asset types, but the file mechanics that decide outcome are consistent across all of them: zoning, servicing, title, regulatory exposure, and the realistic resale or build-out path on the other side.

What this lane covers.

  • Infill, severance, and small assemblies, where the value depends on the planning path and the cost-time profile of getting there.
  • Multi-unit residential, including converted, purpose-built, and add-a-suite scenarios.
  • Income-producing property, including short-term rental holds where the regulatory environment can change the underlying economics quickly.
  • Cottage and recreational property with rental or future development potential, especially in Muskoka, the Kawarthas, and Georgian Bay.
  • Larger parcels with acreage, where the use case is some combination of hold, sever, develop, and exit.

Who this is for.

  • Small-to-mid developers and investors building or expanding a portfolio.
  • Land bankers and assemblers working a multi-year thesis.
  • Multi-unit operators and professional landlords.
  • Family money looking for a development play or a long-term hold.
  • Cottage-country buyers with a rental or future-development thesis, not just a personal-use buy.

What the practice does in this lane.

Investment and development files are won at the offer stage, not at closing. The work that produces a defensible offer and a buildable thesis is the work the practice does up front, before the deposit is committed.

  • Zoning and servicing review. What the by-law actually permits, what variance or amendment would be required, and what the realistic timeline and cost of the planning path looks like.
  • Severance feasibility. Lot sizes, frontages, official-plan alignment, conservation overlap, and the practical question of whether a severance application would clear in this jurisdiction in this market.
  • Title and easements. Encumbrances, registered restrictions, utility rights of way, and anything on title that would constrain the build-out or hold strategy.
  • Multi-unit operations review. Rent roll quality, tenancy compliance, fire and ESA status, work-orders, and the gap between the listed cap rate and the actual one.
  • Cottage and rental-regulation read. Municipal short-term-rental by-laws, conservation authority constraints, septic and well-water regulation, lake-association covenants where they apply.
  • Offer structure. Conditional periods that match the diligence required, deposit terms, closing dates, and the conditions worth insisting on versus those worth trading away.

Geographic focus.

The lane works most actively in the development belt: Halton, Hamilton, and Niagara, where infill and assembly opportunities still exist at workable basis. Whitchurch-Stouffville, Uxbridge, and King for severance and larger-lot files. Cottage country (Muskoka, the Kawarthas, Georgian Bay) for recreational with hold or development thesis. Urban infill across the GTA where the planning path is realistic.

Notes from the practice.

The deeper guide on this lane runs the cap rate, rent roll, expenses, severance feasibility, hold period, and exit questions in the order each one matters.
