How I Work

Six standards.

What every client can expect, and what every transaction is built on.

I

The deal has to make sense for you.

I will not push a transaction because a commission is available. If the downside is real, you will hear it. When the deal is right, I will press it without hesitation.

II

The work runs ahead of the negotiation.

Market context. File. Call. Every clause read, every likely issue mapped, every position understood before the call begins. When the moment to move arrives, the work is already done.

III

Written transaction note before you move.

For engaged clients, properties under active consideration receive a written note: what was reviewed, what appears material, what risks should go to counsel, financing, or tax before you proceed.

IV

The hard parts get handled early.

Assignment terms. Servicing. Estoppels. HST. Conservation overlap. Closing adjustments. Found early, they are part of the structure. Found late, they become the deal’s biggest problems.

V

The transaction does not end at signing.

A signed agreement is the midpoint of the work, not the conclusion. Conditions, extensions, deposits, financing, the small issues that surface between signing and closing. I stay on the file through closing and remain available after.

VI

A small roster, on purpose.

A deliberately limited active roster, typically a small number of files at any one time. That is the level at which every client gets the attention the work requires. New conversations are welcome in any of the four categories.

The deal moves on its soundness, not on the noise around it.

For Clarity

What this practice does not do.

A short list. Stated openly so you know what this practice is for and what it is not.

  • Seller-side listings.

    This practice represents buyers only, by design. Seller-side files, when they come up, are handled on referral to colleagues at HomeLife G1.

  • Multiple representation.

    I represent one side of a transaction, never both. Representing both parties to the same deal divides loyalty and weakens the side that hired me. If the other party needs representation, I refer them to a trusted colleague.

  • Broadcast marketing.

    Growth here is built on relationships, network, and deliberate strategy, not broadcast content and follower counts. Focused by design.

  • Free market valuations as a lead magnet.

    A transaction note is not a comp-pulled price estimate engineered to capture an email. It is a written memo for one specific property, reserved for clients under engagement.

  • Pressure tactics.

    Urgency framing, manufactured competition, "act now or lose this" closing. Decisions get made on the soundness of the deal, not the urgency around it.

  • Showings without preparation.

    Property tours work best when the buyer knows the brief, has financing in place or actively underway, and is positioned to act when the right transaction appears. That is the standard for showings under this practice.
