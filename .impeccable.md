# Primegate — Design Context

This file is the source of truth for design decisions. Every UI/UX skill
(Impeccable, ux-ui-ux-pro-max, downstream `imp-*` skills) reads this
before producing output, so future passes inherit the same direction
without re-asking.

## Design Context

### Users

High-net-worth Ontario real estate clients — HNW individuals, family
offices, founders, executives, professionals, and counsel making
referrals. Sophisticated buyers and sellers transacting in:

- Premium pre-construction (from $1M)
- Investment and development property (multi-unit, income, cottage, raw
  acreage)
- Businesses sold with their real estate (from $2M transaction value)
- Private representation (principals, family-held assets, sensitive
  transactions)

They have already seen every Compass agent page, every Sotheby's
International Realty affiliate, every Engel & Völkers boutique listing
site in Toronto. They are *immune* to drone hero footage, "MEET YOUR
REALTOR" hero modules, and stock-luxury photography. They want a
practitioner who actually reads the documents, not a marketing engine
dressed up to look like one.

Viewing context: read on laptops in offices and on phones late at
night, often when the visitor is already mid-transaction or weighing a
referral. Decisions move fast inside this audience — the page has
roughly thirty seconds to register as a serious counterparty.

### Brand Personality

**Three words: Decisive · Considered · Unhurried.**

- **Decisive.** Says what it does, names what it does not, uses no
  hedging language. The voice of someone who has already made the call
  internally and is telling you the result.
- **Considered.** Every sentence is the result of a deliberation. No
  filler. No "we are trusted advisors helping clients navigate" prose.
  When a stance is stated, the reasoning is one sentence away.
- **Unhurried.** No urgency theatre, no pop-ups, no "limited spots,"
  no countdown timers. The site behaves like a practitioner who is
  busy enough to not chase.

Voice/tone: direct without being cold. Lawyerly precision without the
regulatory affect. Reads like Taran on a call — names the risk, says
what's done, points to where the client's lawyer takes over.

Emotional goal: a visitor should feel that someone has *seen this
transaction shape before* and is not rushing them. The site reassures
through structure, not warmth.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Direction: Brutalist-confident.**

Raw structure on display. Oversized typographic statements. No
decoration. Confidence as the design language itself. The opposite of
champagne-and-gold luxury — closer to a museum exhibit caption set in
stone, or the inside of a rare-book restorer's shop.

**Theme: Dark.** Derived from audience context — most reads happen on
phones late at night during active transactions, where dark surfaces
are easier on the eyes and read as "after-hours private channel" rather
than "consumer site". Bone (warm off-white) text on near-black ink
surfaces. No pure white, no pure black.

**Visual signature** — the one thing a visitor remembers:

A single massive typographic gesture in the hero. The H1 is set at
genuine display scale (clamp from ~80px mobile to ~220px wide
desktop), bleeds toward the viewport edges, and is paired with
extreme-tiny editorial body text below for maximum contrast. The page
introduces itself by saying *one thing* very loudly, then drops to a
confident library voice for everything that follows. No second
crescendo. The hero is the brand artifact.

**Anti-references** (what this redesign explicitly does NOT look like):

- Compass / The Agency / Sotheby's affiliate sites — agent personal
  pages with drone hero video, "MEET YOUR REALTOR" intros, testimonial
  carousels, recent-listings strips, hero photography of the agent in
  a tailored coat against a Toronto backdrop.
- Law firm websites — champagne italic flourishes, "Our Practice"
  formal headings, regulatory tone, Roman numeral commitments,
  centered headlines on cream paper.
- Engel & Völkers / Royal LePage Signature templates — cookie-cutter
  luxury real estate done by a marketing agency.
- AI-generated landing pages — gradient text, glassmorphism cards,
  repeating icon-headline-paragraph blocks, side-stripe accent borders
  on cards, soft drop shadows on rounded rectangles, sparkline
  decorations.
- The previous v3 mock (champagne gold + Fraunces + italic accents) —
  too refined, too editorial-publication, not brutal enough.

### Design Principles

1. **One voice, one moment.** The page speaks once at full volume in
   the hero. Everything that follows is set in a confident library
   voice. No second crescendo, no surprise visual flourishes
   mid-scroll, no second peak of intensity. Editorial pages do this
   well; marketing pages usually fail at it.

2. **Type does the design.** No ornamental SVG flourishes, no
   italic-coloured accents on headlines, no decorative dividers, no
   icon-with-rounded-corners above headings. Visual hierarchy is built
   from scale × weight × negative space. Everything else is invisible
   scaffolding.

3. **Asymmetry over centering.** Left-aligned, ragged-right where
   appropriate. Intentional grid breaks at moments that matter.
   Centered headlines read as templated; off-balance layouts read as
   art-directed.

4. **Specificity beats sophistication.** Named transaction types,
   named risk patterns, named stages, named documents. The site is the
   proof-of-competence itself — readers should leave thinking *this
   person knows this work cold*, not *this person has nice branding*.

5. **Compliance carried prominently, not apologetically.** Brokerage
   attribution (HomeLife G1 Realty Inc., Brokerage), Salesperson
   designation, RECO registration, regulatory disclosure — all present
   and dignified. Not minimised. Not buried at 9pt grey at the bottom.
   Compliance done with confidence reads as professionalism, not
   defensiveness.

6. **No theatre.** No drone hero, no testimonials carousel, no
   "as seen in" press strip, no number counters that animate from
   zero, no auto-playing video, no ambient motion. Movement is
   reserved for state changes only — form submission, link hover, the
   topbar reacting to scroll position. Everything else is still.

### Hard Constraints (regulatory + brand)

These are not design decisions. They cannot be changed by craft mode.

- **Brand display.** Visible brand is "Primegate" alone. Never
  "Primegate Realty," "Primegate Real Estate," "Primegate Group," or
  any entity-suggestive suffix. The domain `primegaterealty.com` is an
  address, not a display surface.
- **Brokerage attribution.** "HomeLife G1 Realty Inc., Brokerage" must
  appear with the "Brokerage" descriptor on every deployed page, in a
  position that satisfies RECO Bulletin 5.3 prominence requirements.
- **Practitioner designation.** "Salesperson" — never "Agent," "Sales
  Representative," "Realtor®" inline (the Realtor® mark requires
  specific usage rules and is held back).
- **No legal-practice references.** Pass 1 (April 2026) removed all
  references to former legal training. They do not return.
- **No misleading-advertising language.** No "guaranteed return,"
  "best deal," "exclusive access" (RECO rule).
- **Em dashes purged from user-visible strings.** Use commas, periods,
  or parentheses.
- **Tech stack.** Static HTML / CSS / SVG. No build step, no
  framework, no npm dependency in deployed assets. Hosted on GitHub
  Pages → primegaterealty.com.

### Tech & Performance

- Plain HTML/CSS/SVG. No framework.
- Webfont strategy: Google Fonts CDN with `display=swap`. Pick fonts
  not in the imp-impeccable reflex_fonts_to_reject list.
- Modern CSS: `oklch()`, `color-mix`, container queries where useful,
  `:has()` for empty-state hiding (already in use).
- WCAG 2.1 AA contrast minimum on all text. AAA on prose where
  reasonable.
- Reduced-motion: `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` honoured for any
  motion that exists.

### What's Already Locked

These exist in the v2 site and should be carried forward unless the
brutalist-confident direction explicitly contradicts them:

- Notes section as the practice's editorial proof-of-work (long-form
  technical pieces). Worth keeping — it's the moat.
- Two-path intake form (Referred + New Introduction) on the same
  Formspree endpoint, distinguished by hidden `intake_path` field.
- Branded `/thanks/` confirmation page after Formspree submission.
- Sticky table-of-contents on long-form articles.
- Cream-paper panel for article body (legibility on long-form prose).
- Auto PDF generation pipeline for Notes (Puppeteer-driven).
- The four practice categories as the structural backbone.
- The six "How We Work" commitments as content (treatment changes;
  text mostly survives).

### What's Up for Reconsideration

The brutalist-confident direction can rework any of these:

- All typography (current: Fraunces + Inter — both banned by the
  Impeccable reflex list).
- The accent colour (current: champagne gold; later experiments:
  burnished bronze; brutalist-confident may use no accent colour at
  all and rely on bone + ink contrast).
- Layout grid and section rhythm.
- Hero composition.
- Lane card flip mechanic (currently 3D card flip; brutalist-confident
  may favour a flat, full-page list or a different reveal).
- Pillar layout (currently 3+3 cards; can be entirely restructured).
- About section composition.
- Footer treatment.
- Any decorative SVG, gold rule, italic accent, or pattern element.
